# The underside survey is joining cords that never meet

I am documenting these spring foundations before any treatment. A cord course is one observed length between two frame-anchor stations; reversing its recorded segments does not make another course. Its posted course tag remains its identity everywhere—anchor names describe the ends but do not replace that tag. A free crossover is only a visual crossing. A lashing records contact between two courses but preserves both identities, while a spring attachment belongs to that spring and course. Shared tacks on the actual chamfered rail are valid anchor stations.

Each coil’s eight-way record needs four distinct anchored courses forming opposite contact pairs in the displayed crosswise, lengthwise, rising-diagonal, and falling-diagonal families. Repeated segments, duplicate contacts, or one looping course cannot increase that coverage. The FRONT and CHAIR LEFT marks on each record control the underside orientation; selection, axis labels, and evidence must continue to refer to the same physical entity after resizing.

Marking one span affected should mark its entire physical course without changing a merely crossed neighbor. Proposing a replacement profile makes every contact on that coil need reinspection. A recorded-profile match requires the same form, free height, wire gauge, top and bottom diameters, and turn count; it is documentation only, not treatment approval. Failed webbing remains in the survey and requires conservator review rather than making its coils disappear.

Survey completion and conservator review are separate. Hard-edge records use the posted number of front-support courses, while a wire-edge record uses its own wire-continuity evidence. The app must only say whether documentation is complete and whether review is indicated; it must never authorize cutting, repair, structural safety, or return to service.
