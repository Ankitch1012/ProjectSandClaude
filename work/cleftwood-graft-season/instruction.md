# The graft board at Marrowfield has stopped telling me the truth

I have been playing Cleftwood on and off for a fortnight, working the neglected
apple row at Marrowfield with Ada Thrale across a grafting season. The game is
lovely and the writing is lovely, and I have almost stopped trusting anything
it shows me. The board, the plan, the notebook and the record disagree with each
other and with the rules the game itself sets out, and the whole thing falls
apart if I make the window smaller.

Please make the season behave the way it says it does.

## What the game says it does

Each visit puts me somewhere in the orchard with Ada and offers a few choices.
The work I do goes onto the graft board and into the season record, and the
season is measured by three separate things: the canopy I have built, my
standing with Ada, and what the orchard is owed. Each runs from nought to
twenty, and each charge lands as the work happens, so a charge that would carry
one of them below nought leaves it sitting at nought.

The board keeps one card for each stock. Cutting and binding a fresh graft puts
a card up at a hold of one, and adds two to the canopy and one to my standing.
Coming back to a stock that already has a card is more tape on that same graft:
it adds one to its hold and one to the canopy, and it does not start a second
card for the same tree.

Taking the tape off for good releases the graft. That adds one to its hold,
three to my standing and one to the canopy. Cutting a graft back to the
rootstock takes four off my standing and adds three to what the orchard is
owed, and leaves its hold where it was. Leaving a graft wrapped for another
week adds one to what the orchard is owed and changes nothing else. Releasing a
graft that is standing cut back at the time settles part of what was owed and
takes two off it; releasing one that is not cut back does not.

Extra tape on a graft that was cut back builds its hold, but it does not put
that stump back in the row — only releasing it does that. And the record keeps
everything: cutting a graft back is a new line in the record, so the day it was
bound is still there afterwards.

The season plan opens and closes visits on the season as it currently stands,
the moment it changes, and a visit that is not open cannot be walked into even
by a link straight to it. Turning back to a visit I have already walked is a
reading, not another day's work: it is noted in the record as a reading, and
beyond that nothing is worked, nothing is charged, the season does not move back
to that day, and nothing opens or closes because of it.

A notebook page holds the whole season. Writing one moves that page on a
revision, and bringing a page back replaces everything with what is on it — the
board, the three standings, the day and light I was on, which visits are open
and which ways of entering the row are open. A window may only write onto the
revision of a page it took up, either by writing that page or by bringing it
back; if the page has been written on since, the save is refused, whatever is on
the page stays there, and the window says so. A window that has never taken a
page up cannot write onto it at all.

The record reads by day, then by the light the work was done in, then by the
order the work actually happened in. Bringing a page back or reopening the
window never reshuffles it, and work done after a page comes back takes its
place after whatever is already written for that day. And the ways the row can
be entered with the county
are worked out from the row as it stands, so one that stops meeting its terms
stops being offered.

## What actually happens

Coming back to a tree I have already worked starts it over instead of building
on it. Its hold drops back to one and I am charged for it as though I had just
cut a fresh graft, so it never adds up to a well-held tree and the visits and
choices that are supposed to open for one never do.

Taking the tape off a graft counts for nothing at all. The record says I did it,
and then nothing else in the game behaves as though I had — the tree still reads
as sitting wrapped at the hold it was bound at.

Cutting a graft back is worse than being charged wrongly. The line in the record
saying when I bound it stops being there, the tree drops off the board
altogether, and the counter and the numbers fall back as though I had never
touched that stock at all. If I re-tape the stump afterwards, the game puts up a
brand-new graft as though nothing had ever happened there.

A visit that a new graft should open stays shut, and only turns up later on.
A visit that should have shut the moment I cut a graft back is still sitting
there open, and it will let me walk straight into it. The ways of entering the
row are worse: once one has been offered it is offered forever, whatever I do to
the row afterwards.

Turning back to a visit I walked days ago is charged as though I had gone out
and done the work again, and it drags the season back to that day. Bringing back
a notebook page gives me the record and the day from that page, but the board,
the three numbers and the plan are all still from where I had got to — a mixture
of two seasons that never existed.

I lost an evening's work to the last one: I had the notebook open in two windows
from the same page, wrote the season down in one, and then the other one wrote
straight over the top of it without a word. And once a season has a fair few
lines in it, anything I do after bringing a page back jumps to the front of its
day in the record instead of taking its place at the end.

## What it looks like in a smaller window

At about nine hundred pixels wide the game stops being playable. The graft board
comes away and sits over the top of the visit, covering Ada's words and the
buttons. The three choices pile up on one another so I cannot tell which line
belongs to which button. What Ada says is cut off partway through. And the strip
with the three standings on it lies across the bottom of the board.

The visit is the thing I am there for, so at that width it should have the room,
with the board and the standings below it where I can read them.
