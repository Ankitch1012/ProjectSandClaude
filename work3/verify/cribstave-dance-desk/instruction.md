# The crib desk will not pass a dance I know is right

I devise Scottish country dances, and I use this desk to check a new crib before
I send it out to be taught. You write the dance down phrase by phrase, the desk
reads it, dances it through, and tells you what is wrong with it.

## What the desk is meant to know

A crib is one line per phrase. Each line opens with the bars it covers, then a
colon, then the figures danced in those bars.

    1-8 : 1s+2s circle 4H round & back

The bars may be written with a hyphen or with an en dash, and the closing bar may
be left off, in which case the phrase runs to the bar before the next one opens,
or to the last bar of the dance if nothing follows it. A phrase too long for one
line carries on over the lines below it, indented, and the whole of it belongs to
the bars at the top. An italic line above the dance may set couples across the
set before the first bar, like `*3s+4s on opposite sides*`.

The rules it checks are the ones any devisor works to.

Every bar is written down exactly once: the first phrase opens on bar 1, the last
closes on the final bar, and each phrase begins on the bar after the one before
it ends.

The figures on a line fill exactly the bars that line claims. Every figure has a
set length and the whole book is printed on the screen beside the crib.

A figure for two couples needs those couples standing next to each other at the
bar it begins, and a figure for one couple at the top needs that couple at the
top at the bar it begins. Where a couple is standing depends on what the earlier
phrases did to them, not on where they began.

It is a four-couple dance danced four times through, so by the last bar the
couple who began at the top must stand second and the couple who began second
must stand at the top, with the third and fourth couples where they were. Any
couple the preamble sent across the set has to be brought back to its own side
before the last bar.

Every fault names the line it was written on and the bar of the figure that is
wrong.

## What it actually does

It will not pass anything. The dances in the library are ones I have taught for
years and it condemns every one of them.

It says bars are missing that are plainly there. I write the phrases 1-8, 9-16,
17-24, 25-32 and it tells me a bar in the middle was never written. When I do
genuinely leave a hole in a dance it names the wrong bar for it, and when two
phrases both lay claim to the same bar it says nothing at all.

It cannot read its own figure book. It tells me it has never heard of
`rights & lefts`, which is printed on the same screen with eight bars against it,
and figures it does accept it counts at half their length, so a phrase that fills
its eight bars is reported as coming up short. Anything I wrote with an en dash
it will not read at all — line after line, it says there are no bars against the
writing. Where a phrase carries onto a second line, the figures on the line above
are dropped and only the last line is counted.

It has no grip on where anybody is standing. It refuses a figure for two couples
who are side by side because of a cast earlier in the dance, and it passes one
written for two couples who are nowhere near each other. It waves through a dance
that shuffles the couples about and leaves them where they started, which cannot
be danced a second time. A dance that sends couples across the set and never
brings them home is passed as sound, while one that does bring them home is told
they finish on the wrong side.

When the trouble is in the middle of a phrase it blames the bar the phrase opens
on rather than the bar of the figure at fault.

Worst of all, it tells me one thing at a time. I put a crib in, it gives me a
single complaint, I answer that, and out comes another. There is no way to see
what is actually wrong with a dance.

## What it looks like

The numbering down the margin has come away from the writing it counts. Further
down a crib the numbers sit between the lines and against the wrong ones, so the
line a fault points at is not the line the desk has marked. The set diagram is
worse — the place headings have slipped off the couples underneath them, so it
shows a couple standing in a place that is not the one written above it.
